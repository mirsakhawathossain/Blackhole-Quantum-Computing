    Novelty: Feasibility boundary: kinematic vs interior-reconstruction tasks¶

    Contents:

    • Dimensions

    Assessed theory: theory-1

    Dimensions¶

    Phenomenon / Effect — Explicit Established¶

    What is Known: Across the set, kinematic/fixed-background signatures (Hawking/Unruh-like thermality, horizon scattering, curved-spacetime propagation) are already experimentally demonstrated on analogue or small quantum-simulator platforms, establishing near-term feasibility under Hamiltonian engineering plus local correlator measurements. Separately, interior-reconstruction/information-recovery tasks (Hayden–Preskill decoding, traversable-wormhole-style protocols as information transfer, Page-curve/interior encoding claims) are known to require substantially stronger operational primitives and exhibit harsh scaling via postselection success probabilities or Grover-like amplification, often needing two-copy entanglement and access to time reversal or U*/U^T. At least one review in the set explicitly elevates this as an organizing distinction between what labs can currently demonstrate versus what remains out of reach at macroscopic black-hole scaling.

    What is Introduced: The statement asserts, as an operational regularity, a feasibility boundary that partitions “black-hole simulation” claims into two classes—(i) a kinematic class feasible on near-term hardware with engineered mapped Hamiltonians and local measurements, and (ii) a reconstruction/decoding class whose realization and verification require additional capabilities: two-copy EPR/TFD resources, controllable implementation of U* or time reversal, postselection or deep amplification with rapidly decreasing success (or increasing runtime), and a verifiable radiation-subsystem partition.

    What is Novel: As a global phenomenon/effect claim, the boundary is not novel relative to this aggregated literature: multiple papers already instantiate the kinematic feasibility side, and decoding-focused work explicitly demonstrates the reconstruction side’s harsher resource/success scaling and required primitives; moreover, one review already states essentially this two-class feasibility separation as a central organizing observation. What remains comparatively novel in many individual primary experiments is the explicit cross-platform naming and packaging of this boundary as a single law-like feasibility taxonomy, but the existence/content of the split is already established in the provided set.

    Explanatory / Mechanistic — Explicit Established¶

    What is Known: Multiple papers already make explicit the key operational/mechanistic ingredients behind Hayden–Preskill decoding and related wormhole/teleportation protocols: the need for two-copy entanglement resources (EPR/TFD), access to conjugate/time-reversed dynamics (U*, U^T), and the use of postselection versus Grover-like amplitude amplification with unfavorable scaling of success probability/iterations. These are treated as established mechanisms in the decoding-focused literature and are not merely implied.

    What is Introduced: The statement introduces an explicit feasibility boundary framed for quantum-hardware claims: it separates “kinematic” fixed-background QFT signatures (achievable with Hamiltonian engineering and local correlator measurements) from “reconstruction/decoding” tasks (interior reconstruction/unitary evaporation demonstrations) that additionally require the above primitives plus an experimentally verifiable partition identifying radiation subsystems. It presents these as two operational classes with distinct resource bottlenecks and verification burdens.

    What is Novel: Because the decoding papers already explicitly analyze two-copy requirements, U*/U^T access, and postselection/amplification scaling, the core mechanistic resource list is not novel relative to that subliterature. The more novel contribution supported by the aggregation is the cross-context operationalization: packaging these ingredients into a general, hardware-oriented checklist/feasibility split that applies across disparate “black-hole simulation” platforms (especially kinematic/analog QFT simulators and general curved-spacetime simulation papers where such a boundary is not stated), with particular added emphasis on verifiable radiation partitioning as a practical bottleneck.

    Unification — Explicit Established¶

    What is Known: Across the assessed literature, the resource/feasibility gap between (i) kinematic analogue/QFT-on-fixed-background signatures and (ii) decoding/interior-reconstruction-style tasks is already explicitly discussed, including the need for TFD/EPR-like two-copy entanglement, time-reversal or implementing U* (or related inverses), and postselection or Grover-like amplification in Hayden–Preskill / traversable-wormhole-inspired protocols. Reviews explicitly unify scrambling/OTOCs, Hayden–Preskill decoding, and traversable-wormhole teleportation into a shared operational circuit/resource picture, making the two-regime narrative broadly established.

    What is Introduced: The statement packages these known distinctions into an explicit operational feasibility boundary with two named classes (“kinematic” vs “reconstruction/decoding”), and provides a consolidated checklist of the dominant extra capabilities and verification burdens for the reconstruction class (two-copy resources, U*/time reversal, postselection/amplification scaling, and verifiable radiation partition).

    What is Novel: As a unification claim, the separation and main bottlenecks appear to be already explicit and treated as established knowledge in cross-cutting treatments; the novelty is mainly rhetorical/organizational (a bundled boundary/checklist) rather than a new unifying principle. However, the inclusion of an explicitly “experimentally verifiable radiation partition” as part of the feasibility boundary is less foregrounded in some sources and may be an elevation of a usually implicit verification burden rather than a wholly new idea.

    Generalization / Scope Expansion — Derivable Unstated¶

    What is Known: Across the literature, analogue/kinematic black-hole simulations establish that horizon/Unruh/Hawking-like signatures can be accessed by engineering an effective Hamiltonian/metric mapping and measuring relatively local correlators, while separate holography/quantum-information lines (Hayden–Preskill decoding, traversable-wormhole teleportation, islands/entanglement-wedge reconstruction) study “interior/recovery” tasks that typically require more structured resources and are demonstrated only in idealized or small-N settings. Reviews and proposals discuss feasibility, finite-size caveats, and measurement difficulty, but usually in platform- or protocol-specific terms rather than as a single cross-platform feasibility boundary tied to a concrete capability checklist.

    What is Introduced: The statement makes an explicit, device-agnostic scope claim: near-term hardware can plausibly realize kinematic fixed-background QFT signatures given Hamiltonian engineering plus local correlator access, whereas interior reconstruction/evaporation-unitarity tasks form a distinct class requiring additional operational capabilities (two-copy EPR/TFD resources, controllable time reversal or implementation of U* / U^T, postselection or deep amplification/decoding with rapidly decreasing success probability, and an experimentally verifiable radiation partition). It therefore expands feasibility reasoning from individual platforms into a general taxonomy meant to apply across analogue horizons, digital simulations, and holographic/information-theoretic protocols (including NISQ vs prospective fault-tolerant regimes).

    What is Novel: The aggregated paper evidence suggests the ingredients are mostly present but scattered: decoding-focused works analyze postselection/amplification scaling and asymptotic-vs-small-N gaps, while analogue/OTOC/platform papers discuss what is measurable/feasible for kinematic diagnostics and highlight practical constraints. What is not typically explicit in any one paper is the consolidated, prescriptive cross-platform split into two operational feasibility classes with a specific capability/verification checklist for when “reconstruction/decoding” claims become meaningful; several assessed papers treat this broad cross-regime feasibility boundary as extending beyond their stated scope. However, because multiple reviews already gesture at cross-platform prospects and scaling caveats, the novelty is best characterized as a synthesized generalization that is largely derivable from the existing (dispersed) literature rather than wholly unprecedented.

    Constraint / Limitation — Explicit Established¶

    What is Known: Across the assessed literature, the key resource/verification obstacles for interior-reconstruction/decoding-style claims are already explicitly stated—often with quantitative scaling: two-copy EPR/TFD resources, access to backward evolution or U*/U^T, and postselection/amplification costs with rapidly degrading success as message size grows; multiple sources also emphasize the need for a well-defined, operationally verifiable ‘radiation’ subsystem and note ensemble-vs-single-instance/factorization issues when comparing to gravitational path-integral predictions. Reviews further draw a fairly explicit distinction between kinematic fixed-background QFT analogues (local correlators/thermality) and stronger information-recovery/interior claims with heavier requirements.

    What is Introduced: The statement consolidates these known constraints into a clean operational ‘feasibility boundary’ split between a kinematic class (engineer mapped Hamiltonian + measure local correlators) and a reconstruction/decoding class (requires two-copy resources, U* or time reversal, postselection/amplification scaling, and verifiable radiation partition), framed explicitly for near-term hardware claims. Relative to many kinematic analogue and curved-spacetime simulation papers, it adds a reconstruction-focused checklist that those works typically do not enumerate in one place.

    What is Novel: Relative to the full assessed corpus, the constraint content is not substantively novel: decoding-focused theory papers and broad reviews already explicitly derive/state essentially the same necessary ingredients and unfavorable scaling, including the partition/factorization caveat and (in several sources) ensemble/typicality qualifications. The main contribution is an organizational repackaging/operationalization for evaluating ‘black-hole simulation’ claims across platforms, which is useful rhetorically and procedurally but does not outweigh the presence of explicit, established prior articulations in key reconstruction/decoding references.

    Conceptual Reframing / Abstraction — Derivable Unstated¶

    What is Known: Across the assessed literature, many works already frame particular “black-hole simulation” efforts operationally in practice (choose a target diagnostic like Hawking/Unruh spectra, OTOCs, or teleportation correlators; specify a mapping to hardware; specify measurement protocols), and several explicitly contrast analogue/kinematic horizon signatures with holographic/quantum-information tasks such as scrambling and decoding. In particular, the scrambling review explicitly organizes experiments around concrete observables and platform mappings and contrasts kinematic analogues with holographic/information-theoretic analogues.

    What is Introduced: The statement elevates these dispersed practices into a prescriptive feasibility-boundary taxonomy: near-term hardware can access a “kinematic class” with engineered Hamiltonians and local correlator measurements, while “reconstruction/decoding” tasks additionally require two-copy entanglement resources (EPR/TFD), controllable implementation of time reversal or U*/U^T, postselection or deep amplification/decoding with unfavorable scaling, and a verifiable radiation-partition. In the broader theory context used in the paper-level assessments, this boundary is intended to sit inside a named operational-equivalence-class lens (O,M,W) as the normative way to state and compare simulation claims.

    What is Novel: The split between kinematic analogue signatures and harder information-theoretic/interior-reconstruction goals, and the identification of key resource bottlenecks (two-copy access/TFD, U* or reversal, postselection/Grover-like amplification) is largely consistent with themes already present across multiple papers, so the main novelty is packaging them as a single explicit, normative “feasibility boundary” principle tied to an operational-equivalence-class adjudication lens. Aggregated assessments contain no strong evidence that this exact prescriptive taxonomy is explicitly established as a named principle; instead it appears mostly as a synthesis/abstraction that is straightforward to infer from prior discussions but typically unstated as a single organizing rule.

    Empirical Synthesis / Meta-Regularity — Explicit Established¶

    What is Known: Across the assessed literature, it is already explicitly articulated (especially in review/synthesis papers) that near-term platforms can robustly access kinematic/fixed-background signatures (e.g., Hawking/Unruh-like thermality, horizon scattering, local correlators, OTOCs), whereas interior-reconstruction/evaporation-unitarity style claims (teleportation-through-wormhole, Hayden–Preskill decoding, Page-curve validation) require substantially stronger resources and face harsh scaling and verification burdens. Reviews also explicitly discuss concrete bottlenecks such as preparing two-copy entanglement resources (EPR/TFD), implementing time reversal or U*/U^T-type operations, and the rapidly degrading success/fidelity of decoding/postselection-style protocols with increasing problem size.

    What is Introduced: The statement packages these recurring observations into a single operational feasibility boundary that splits “black-hole simulation” into (1) a kinematic class, feasible given Hamiltonian engineering plus local measurements, and (2) a reconstruction/decoding class, requiring (i) two-copy entanglement resources, (ii) controllable time reversal / implementation of U* or U^T, (iii) postselection or deep amplification/decoding with typically rapidly falling success probability, and (iv) an experimentally verifiable ‘radiation’ subsystem partition. It frames this as a meta-regularity about resource bottlenecks and verification burdens that delimit credible experimental claims on near-term hardware.

    What is Novel: As an across-paper meta-regularity, the kinematic-vs-reconstruction feasibility split is not overall novel because at least one dedicated scrambling/quantum-simulator review already explicitly synthesizes this exact kind of near-term feasibility boundary and its scaling limitations. Relative to many single-platform experimental papers (analogue Hawking/Unruh demonstrations, single mapping studies) and single-program theoretical proposals (SYK hardware proposals, specific teleportation simulations), the claim is largely an explicit synthesis that is derivable but typically unstated there; however, the existence of explicit review-level statements weighs heavily toward this being established rather than new.

    Novelty: Mechanism certification criterion: coherent size-winding is a discriminant for holographic-style teleportation¶

    Contents:

    • Dimensions

    Assessed theory: theory-6

    Dimensions¶

    Phenomenon / Effect — Explicit Established¶

    What is Known: In the most directly relevant sources in this set, the central empirical ingredients are already described: the distinction between “peak-size” vs “size-winding” teleportation mechanisms in SYK-like protocols, the approximately linear-in-size phase of a complex size-resolved quantity q(l) with strong intra-size coherence, and the idea that an appropriate two-sided coupling can effectively “unwind” those phases. Additionally, at least one finite-N/hardware-oriented SYK study explicitly measures q(l) and R(l) and links μ-sign–dependent slope reversal of arg q(l) to sign-asymmetric mutual-information/teleportation performance.

    What is Introduced: The statement consolidates these ingredients into a single sufficient operational discriminator/certification criterion with three explicit conditions: (1) linear arg q(l) over dominant P(l) support, (2) R(l)≈1 over that support, and (3) strong μ-sign asymmetry with enhancement when μ cancels the winding. It also adds a prescriptive interpretive rule: teleportation-like signals lacking (1)–(2) should not be attributed to the size-winding (holographic) mechanism, but instead to alternative mechanisms such as peaked-size/thermalization-driven interference.

    What is Novel: Relative to the most relevant literature here, the underlying effect claims (existence of coherent size-winding, its relation to unwinding by coupling choice, and associated sign-asymmetries) are not novel: they are already explicitly discussed in the review-level treatment and operationalized/observed in the sparse-SYK teleportation simulation/experiment paper. Any remaining novelty is mainly in packaging/elevating a bundle of already-described diagnostics into a formalized, standalone “sufficient discriminator” and an explicit admonition about attribution, which reads as synthesis rather than discovery of a new phenomenon.

    Explanatory / Mechanistic — Explicit Peripheral¶

    What is Known: Across the assessed SYK/teleportation-focused literature, the core “teleportation-by-size-winding” mechanism is already articulated: a two-sided coupling can imprint size-dependent phases, and in holographic/SYK-like regimes the phase of a size-resolved quantity is approximately linear in size and can be cancelled/enhanced by the appropriate sign of the coupling, yielding strong sign-asymmetry in teleportation metrics. It is also known that teleportation-like signatures can arise from non-holographic mechanisms (e.g., narrowly peaked size distributions, integrable/oscillatory or thermalization-driven interference), so teleportation success alone is not a unique holographic indicator.

    What is Introduced: The statement packages these ingredients into an explicit operational “mechanism certification” rule with three jointly checked conditions: (1) approximate linearity of arg q(l) in size l over the dominant support of P(l), (2) an explicit intra-size coherence requirement R(l)=|q(l)|/P(l)≈1 over that support, and (3) a causal/diagnostic link between flipping the sign of μ, reversal/cancellation of the winding slope, and a correspondingly strong sign-asymmetry in teleportation figures of merit. It additionally makes a normative attribution claim: if (1)–(2) fail, observed teleportation should not be attributed to the size-winding (holographic) mechanism.

    What is Novel: The underlying mechanistic picture (linear size-dependent phases, cancellation by coupling sign, and alternative peak-size/thermalization mechanisms) appears explicitly and centrally in at least one broad review, and is analyzed in detail (including competing mechanisms and μ-sign behavior) in commuting/nonchaotic variants, reducing novelty of the basic mechanism. What remains novel is mainly the formalization into a sufficient operational discriminator with an explicit coherence-ratio test R(l)≈1 and a stronger “certification/attribution” reading (a practical decision rule rather than a descriptive mechanism), which is not stated as an established theorem across the assessed works.

    Unification — Explicit Established¶

    What is Known: Across the assessed corpus, key building blocks are already present: (i) operator-size diagnostics (including size-resolved complex quantities like q(l) and coherence measures like R(l)) in SYK-like teleportation simulations, and (ii) the explicit idea that teleportation-like signatures can arise from multiple distinct mechanisms (holographic/size-winding versus non-holographic mimics such as peaked-size interference or thermalization), requiring discrimination. In particular, a major scrambling/teleportation review and the Commuting-SYK work already treat this mechanism-level taxonomy and contrast as an explicit, central organizing theme rather than an unarticulated synthesis.

    What is Introduced: The theory statement packages these ingredients into a single operational certification criterion: require (1) approximately linear phase arg q(l) across dominant support of P(l), (2) near-unity coherence ratio R(l) over that support, and (3) a strong sign-asymmetric response under μ→−μ in the two-sided coupling e^{iμV}, with enhancement when the coupling cancels the winding. It further elevates the negative claim into the same rule: teleportation signals without (1)–(2) should not be attributed to the size-winding (holographic) mechanism and are instead more consistent with alternative mechanisms.

    What is Novel: Relative to many broad reviews/white papers and adjacent black-hole/QI syntheses that discuss wormholes, scrambling, teleportation, or experimental platforms without proposing a single microscopic discriminator, this explicit ‘one-rule’ operator-size-based diagnostic is a new cross-cutting unification. However, the least-novel evidence is decisive for the core claim of unification: the review and Commuting-SYK paper already contain essentially the same conceptual unification (holographic size-winding vs non-holographic mimics) and the need to distinguish them, so the novelty here is primarily in explicit operationalization/packaging rather than in introducing a new unifying principle in the most directly relevant subliterature.

    Generalization / Scope Expansion — Derivable Unstated¶

    What is Known: Within the core two-sided SYK traversable-wormhole-teleportation literature, operator-size notions (Pauli/Majorana strings) and the use of two-sided couplings are already in-scope, and small-N implementations are explicitly explored. Reviews and in-model analyses also already emphasize that small-N, commuting, or learned/structured Hamiltonians can produce “teleportation-like” signatures without uniquely certifying a holographic mechanism.

    What is Introduced: The statement packages these ingredients into a portable operational certification criterion with explicit applicability conditions: (i) a meaningful operator-size decomposition, (ii) size-resolved phase coherence quantified by linear arg q(l) and R(l)≈1 over dominant support, and (iii) a μ-sign reversal test tied to cancellation of size-dependent phase winding, intended to be used across SYK-like models including small-N digital implementations. It also explicitly delimits where it should not be applied (e.g., analogue-gravity Hawking/Unruh experiments lacking two-sided entangled copies and size-based couplings) and foregrounds practical measurement caveats/proxies.

    What is Novel: Across most of the provided corpus (general scrambling/OTOC measurement papers, SYK platform proposals, black-hole information papers, and holography/channel-capacity discussions), this explicit cross-setting certification scope and its “do-not-attribute-without-(1)-(2)” rule is not stated and thus represents a genuine scope expansion relative to those works. However, in the closest adjacent sources (a scrambling review and a commuting-SYK analysis), the logic that size structure and small-N/commuting mimicry limit holographic interpretation is already present, making the criterion’s broad scope and boundary-setting largely an explicit elevation/packaging of derivable implications rather than entirely new content in that near literature.

    Constraint / Limitation — Explicit Established¶

    What is Known: Across the provided literature, it is already explicitly emphasized (especially in the scrambling/teleportation review and the Commuting-SYK analysis) that teleportation-like signatures (mutual information/fidelity improvements) are not, by themselves, a reliable certificate of holographic/wormhole dynamics: small-N effects, nonchaotic/commuting/learned Hamiltonians, peaked-size distributions, and generic thermalization can mimic similar signals. The Commuting-SYK paper further explicitly discusses regimes where size-winding is present or washed out and notes the exponential/tomographic difficulty of directly accessing fine-grained size-resolved quantities such as q(l) and coherence measures.

    What is Introduced: The statement packages these interpretational caveats into a more prescriptive operational certification rule: attribute teleportation to the holographic size-winding mechanism only if (1) arg q(l) is approximately linear in size over the dominant support of P(l), (2) the coherence ratio R(l)=|q(l)|/P(l) is near 1 over that support, and (3) flipping the sign of μ in G(μ)=e^{iμV} reverses/cancels the winding and yields a strong sign-asymmetry in teleportation metrics, with enhancement for the canceling sign. It also asserts a negative diagnostic: teleportation observed without (1)–(2) should not be attributed to size-winding and is more consistent with alternative mechanisms.

    What is Novel: When aggregating across the paper set, the core limitation claim (“teleportation signals alone do not certify holographic wormhole dynamics; mimics exist”) is not novel because it is explicitly established and treated as central in both the general review and the Commuting-SYK work. The added novelty is mainly in elevating specific size-resolved phase-coherence conditions (linearity of arg q(l), high R(l)) plus the μ-sign reversal test into an explicit sufficient discriminator/decision criterion; however, the existence and experimental relevance of these kinds of size-winding/coherence caveats and mechanism competition are already explicitly discussed in the Commuting-SYK analysis, so overall this dimension is best judged as established rather than new within this corpus.

    Conceptual Reframing / Abstraction — Explicit Peripheral¶

    What is Known: Within the closest SYK-teleportation/operator-growth literature in the provided set, the operator-size framing and the distinction between “size-winding” versus “peaked-size/thermalization” mechanisms is already explicitly articulated, and concrete size-resolved complex diagnostics and ratios (q(l), R(l), Qn/Pn, rn) are already defined and used interpretively. In particular, a sparse/chaotic SYK teleportation simulation paper explicitly formalizes q(l) and R(l) and applies them as mechanism probes, and a “commuting SYK” paper similarly operationalizes phase structure/ratios as diagnostics for pseudo-holographic teleportation signatures; a broad review also already adopts the operator-size/winding-vs-peak-size organizing lens.

    What is Introduced: The statement packages these ingredients into an explicit operational “certification criterion” framed as a sufficient discriminator: (1) approximately linear arg q(l) over the dominant support of P(l), (2) near-unity intra-size phase coherence R(l)=|q(l)|/P(l) on that support, and (3) a correlated strong μ-sign asymmetry where flipping sign reverses/cancels the winding slope and correspondingly enhances/suppresses teleportation figures of merit. It also elevates the interpretive rule “teleportation without (1)–(2) should not be attributed to size-winding” as the key abstraction, i.e., a mechanism-classification lens rather than treating teleportation peaks alone as evidence.

    What is Novel: Aggregating across papers, the dominant limiting evidence comes from the most relevant SYK-teleportation papers: they already contain essentially the same operator-size phase/coherence objects (q(l)/R(l) or close analogues) and use them diagnostically, which makes the present contribution largely a consolidation/standardization into a single triad-style operational test rather than a new abstraction. Relative to many broader black-hole/chaos/analogue-gravity/channel-framing works in the set, the operator-size phase-coherence diagnostic lens is absent and thus looks “new” there; however, that does not outweigh the explicit prior-art overlap in the immediate mechanism-diagnostics subliterature.

    Empirical Synthesis / Meta-Regularity — Derivable Unstated¶

    What is Known: Within the SYK/teleportation literature in this set, it is already recognized that (i) teleportation-like figures of merit can arise via multiple mechanisms (size-winding vs peaked-size/thermalization) and thus are not, by themselves, a certificate of holographic wormhole dynamics, and (ii) in specific SYK-like settings one can observe size-winding diagnostics and μ-sign-dependent asymmetries (at least within a model/ensemble). A particularly direct prior synthesis (Commuting SYK) already articulates the need to disambiguate mechanisms using size/phase diagnostics rather than teleportation performance alone.

    What is Introduced: The statement packages these ingredients into a concrete, sufficient, multi-part operational discriminator intended to be applied across implementations: existence of a time/coupling window where arg q(l) is approximately linear across the dominant support of P(l), the coherence ratio R(l)=|q(l)|/P(l) is near 1 over that support, and flipping the sign of μ reverses/cancels the winding and yields a strong sign-asymmetry/enhancement in teleportation metrics. It further asserts a negative diagnostic: teleportation signals observed without (1)–(2) should not be attributed to the size-winding mechanism.

    What is Novel: Aggregating across papers, the main novelty is not the individual components (size-winding diagnostics; μ-sign asymmetry; multiple mechanisms for teleportation-like signals) but the explicit triad-as-a-rule and its elevation to a cross-study certification criterion. The closest relevant work (Commuting SYK) substantially supports the same practical conclusion and mechanism taxonomy, making the proposed criterion best viewed as a derivable-but-previously-unstated meta-regularity/operationalization rather than a clearly new empirical law established by the provided literature; relative to more distant reviews/theory/analogue-gravity papers, the criterion appears genuinely new simply because they do not address these size-resolved teleportation diagnostics.

    Novelty: Holographic-information simulations require many-body entanglement and scrambling validation, not horizons¶

    Contents:

    • Dimensions

    Assessed theory: theory-4

    Dimensions¶

    Phenomenon / Effect — Explicit Established¶

    What is Known: Across holography/SYK/JT and quantum-simulation reviews and demonstrations, “Track B”-style goals are already operationalized by many-body information diagnostics (OTOCs/scrambling, teleportation fidelity or mutual information signatures, Page-curve/Page-time transitions, reconstruction/decoding maps) and by resource requirements such as TFD/EPR-like entanglement and two-sided couplings/decoders. Multiple sources also make clear that these diagnostics can be implemented and measured on non-gravitational platforms, i.e., without engineering an explicit horizon-like effective metric. In addition, there is explicit evidence that some headline Track-B-style signatures (e.g., traversable-wormhole teleportation signals) can occur as false positives in non-chaotic/integrable models, motivating explicit scrambling/chaos validation rather than “horizon-like” reasoning.

    What is Introduced: The statement consolidates as a single empirical/operational claim that Track B simulation “success” is defined by many-body information observables plus special entangled-state preparation and decoding/two-sided operations, and that none of this requires any explicit horizon/metric engineering. It also implicitly elevates a methodological stance: do not treat horizon analogues as relevant validation criteria for Track B targets.

    What is Novel: Relative to the surveyed literature, the core content (Track B defined by information observables/resources rather than horizons) is already explicit and established in reviews and primary holography/SYK-teleportation work; novelty is therefore low. The most distinctive strengthening supported by the provided evidence is the “false-positive” pressure: commuting/integrable SYK can show teleportation-like signals despite slow/non-chaotic dynamics, sharpening the need for independent scrambling/chaos validation beyond any single signature. That sharpening is better viewed as an added constraint/interpretation emphasis rather than a new phenomenon.

    Explanatory / Mechanistic — Explicit Established¶

    What is Known: Across core holography/black-hole-information and quantum-simulation literature, Track-B ingredients are already explicitly treated as the operative mechanisms/diagnostics: TFD/two-sided entanglement, scrambling/OTOCs, decoding/reconstruction maps (e.g., Petz/modular flow), and Page/QES-type transitions; several sources also explicitly connect these to traversable-wormhole teleportation protocols rather than to analogue horizon engineering. Some quantum-simulation/chaos papers discuss OTOC measurement and time-reversal operations, but may not elevate the full Track-B “resource checklist” to a central principle.

    What is Introduced: The statement packages these ingredients into an explicit operational success criterion for “holographic/information-theoretic” simulations: validate via many-body information observables and require special entangled resources plus two-sided couplings/decoding/time-reversal operations, while asserting that no explicit horizon-like metric engineering is needed for these tasks.

    What is Novel: Relative to review/theory papers that already articulate the TFD–wormhole, scrambling–OTOC, and reconstruction/decoding mechanisms, the statement is largely a restatement (low novelty). The most novel element is the explicit, lab-facing contrast claim (“not horizons”) and the elevation of a necessity-style operational checklist, which is only implicit or peripheral in some simulator-focused papers; however, this contrast is still broadly derivable from the juxtaposition of analogue-horizon (Track A) versus holographic-information (Track B) literatures rather than being a new mechanism.

    Unification — Derivable Unstated¶

    What is Known: Across the assessed corpus, Track-B-style black-hole/holography simulations are already explicitly framed in terms of many-body information diagnostics and resources—e.g., scrambling/OTOCs and chaos bounds, teleportation fidelity/channel capacity, Page-curve/island/QES logic, and decoding/reconstruction maps often tied to TFD/EPR-type entanglement—without requiring any analogue metric horizon engineering. At the same time, most papers do not explicitly elevate a single cross-program operational rule that ‘Track B success is defined by information observables and entanglement resources rather than horizons’ as a named organizing taxonomy separating it from analogue-horizon work.

    What is Introduced: The statement explicitly unifies diverse holographic-information demonstrations under an operational checklist (OTOCs/teleportation mutual information/Page-like transitions/reconstruction maps plus special entangled resources and decoding/two-sided couplings) and makes the horizon-independence of these tasks part of the success criterion. In context of the broader dual-track theory, it also functions as one half of a partitioning principle intended to prevent importing Track-A (horizon/spectrum) validation into Track-B (information/reconstruction) claims.

    What is Novel: The Track-B ingredients themselves (scrambling diagnostics, TFD/ER=EPR motivation, decoding/teleportation observables) are largely explicit and established in review and core theory papers; thus that content is not strongly novel. The more novel contribution is the explicit, prescriptive operational separation—stating at the principle level that Track-B validation is fundamentally horizon-independent and should be judged by many-body information observables/resources—which appears in the assessed set mostly as a synthesis that can be inferred from surveys/juxtapositions rather than as an explicitly stated, widely recognized taxonomy; ambiguity results (e.g., pseudo-/commuting-SYK producing “teleportation-like” signals) support the need for track-specific validation but do not by themselves supply this overarching rule.

    Generalization / Scope Expansion — Explicit Established¶

    What is Known: Multiple Track-B (holographic/information-theoretic) papers and reviews already explicitly treat “black-hole-like” simulation targets as many-body boundary dynamics (e.g., SYK/JT-inspired models, random circuits) to be validated by information observables (OTOCs/scrambling, teleportation fidelity, Page-curve/island/QES-style transitions), rather than by engineering an analogue metric or horizon. Several sources also explicitly discuss the practical resource requirements for these diagnostics (special entangled states like TFD/EPR resources, two-sided couplings, decoding/time-reversal-style operations) and show that similar signatures can occur even in atypical model classes (e.g., integrable/commuting SYK variants).

    What is Introduced: The statement consolidates this into an operational scope principle: for Track-B simulations, success criteria are defined by many-body information observables and the associated preparation/operation requirements, and these demonstrations can be realized without any explicit horizon-like metric engineering. It further sharpens the “not horizons” separation as a validation rule (i.e., horizons are not the right success criterion for Track B).

    What is Novel: As a scope/generalization claim, the core content is largely not novel because it is already explicit and central in key Track-B simulation proposals and surveys (digital minimal AdS/CFT; scrambling-to-simulators reviews), and is also consistent with the broader holography literature that treats information diagnostics in non-geometric boundary models (e.g., SYK evidence alongside JT/gravity toy models). The incremental novelty is mainly emphasis/packaging: elevating an operationally crisp separation criterion (“validate by information observables, not horizons”) and bundling concrete implementation-resource requirements and known confounders (e.g., teleportation-like signals in commuting/integrable or small-N/sparse instances) into a single statement; this latter consolidation is present but more peripheral/uneven across individual papers.

    Constraint / Limitation — Explicit Established¶

    What is Known: Across core holography/information-paradox works, it is explicitly established that kinematic/thermal (fixed-background) signatures are insufficient to certify unitarity or interior reconstruction, and that entanglement structure and code-subspace/replica-wormhole conditions constrain what “interior access” can mean. Separately, scrambling/OTOC-style diagnostics are widely treated as at best necessary-not-sufficient for holography, and specific counterexamples (e.g., commuting/non-chaotic SYK variants) show that some “gravity-like” signals can be false positives.

    What is Introduced: The statement crystallizes these points into an operational validation constraint for Track-B-style (holographic/information-theoretic) simulations: success should be defined by many-body information observables and protocol resources (e.g., TFD/EPR-like entanglement, two-sided couplings/decoding, and chaos/scrambling diagnostics) and should not depend on, or be inferred from, explicit horizon/metric engineering. It also makes the methodological warning explicit: do not cross-apply Track-A horizon/thermal evidence as Track-B evidence (and conversely do not infer horizons from teleportation-like Track-B signals).

    What is Novel: As a constraint on what counts as evidence for holographic/information-theoretic claims, the core content is largely not novel relative to the established holography/interior-reconstruction literature that already denies “thermality implies interior reconstruction” and emphasizes entanglement/replica/coding structure. The remaining novelty is mostly in packaging: making a concise, cross-platform, experiment-facing rule about diagnostic separation and false-positive risks (especially emphasizing small-N/commuting/sparse mimics) that many simulation/analogue papers only imply via scattered caveats rather than state as an explicit principle.

    Conceptual Reframing / Abstraction — Explicit Established¶

    What is Known: Across the assessed literature on holography/SYK/wormhole-teleportation and black-hole information, Track-B-style claims are already framed as being validated by many-body information diagnostics (e.g., OTOCs/chaos, teleportation-channel measures, Page/QES transitions, reconstruction/decoding maps) and by resource requirements such as special entangled states (often TFD/EPR) plus couplings/decoders. Multiple papers explicitly treat these information-theoretic tasks as not requiring any engineered analogue metric/horizon, in contrast to analogue-gravity/kinematic-horizon programs that validate via local spectra/correlations.

    What is Introduced: The statement packages this already-common practice into an explicit, prescriptive rule for interpreting and designing ‘holographic-information’ simulations: success is defined by demonstrating scrambling/decoding/teleportation/Page-type observables and the requisite entanglement/coupling operations, and the presence of a horizon-like engineered metric is neither required nor an appropriate validation proxy. It also sharpens the interpretational boundary by implying that importing horizon-style validation criteria into Track B (or vice versa) risks category errors/false positives.

    What is Novel: The core content (“Track B is about information observables and entanglement/scrambling resources, not horizon engineering”) is largely already explicit in several holography/quantum-information papers and is at least implicit as standard validation practice in others, so it is not a new scientific concept per se. The incremental novelty lies mainly in elevating this into a clean, track-specific operational criterion and making the ‘not horizons’ exclusion explicit as part of a broader taxonomy; some papers only gesture at this separation peripherally (e.g., warning that certain signals are not uniquely geometric).

    Empirical Synthesis / Meta-Regularity — Explicit Established¶

    What is Known: Multiple synthesis/review and cross-model theory papers already explicitly articulate that ‘holographic-information’ or information-paradox-relevant behavior is operationally diagnosed by information-theoretic observables (e.g., OTOCs/scrambling, decoding/teleportation fidelity, Page-curve/entropy transitions, reconstruction maps) and depends on special entanglement resources (often TFD/EPR-like) and multi-copy/two-sided/decoding operations. These same sources also emphasize that kinematic/horizon-like or thermal/near-horizon QFT signatures are not sufficient evidence for interior reconstruction or Hayden–Preskill-type information recovery, which instead track entanglement-wedge/QES/replica-wormhole structure and scrambling dynamics.

    What is Introduced: The statement packages this established operational viewpoint into an explicit cross-implementation validation rule for Track B: judge success by many-body information observables and required entanglement/decoding resources, and treat horizon/metric engineering as unnecessary for realizing these information-theoretic tasks. It also implicitly functions as a checklist-style prescription for experimental interpretation (what to measure/prepare) rather than a new empirical finding.

    What is Novel: Given the strongest counterevidence, the core meta-regularity claimed by the statement (Track-B success hinges on many-body entanglement/scrambling/decoding validators and can be realized without explicit horizons) is already explicitly stated and treated as established in key synthesis sources. Any residual novelty is mainly rhetorical/organizational (framing as a ‘track’ rule and emphasizing misvalidation risk), which is not a new cross-paper empirical regularity relative to the most relevant prior syntheses.

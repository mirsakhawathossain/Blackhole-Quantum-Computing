    Novelty: Kinematic-horizon simulations require metric engineering, not decoding primitives¶

    Contents:

    • Dimensions

    Assessed theory: theory-4

    Dimensions¶

    Phenomenon / Effect — Explicit Established¶

    What is Known: Across analogue/kinematic-horizon and QFT-in-curved-space simulation works, Hawking/Unruh-like spectra and related local correlators are routinely treated as kinematic effects that can be produced by engineering an effective metric/horizon (e.g., flow/sound-speed or site-dependent hopping/flux profiles) and measured locally in a single system. Multiple papers explicitly demonstrate Hawking-like emission/signatures in platforms (BECs, superconducting circuits, lattice Hamiltonians) without invoking thermofield-double (TFD) preparation, two-copy resources, or decoding/U* access, and often frame these experiments as fixed-background (non-backreacting) analogues.

    What is Introduced: The statement elevates an operational feasibility claim: for Track A, the dominant requirements are metric/coupling engineering plus sufficient readout resolution of local spectra/correlators, rather than information-theoretic primitives used in holographic/interior-reconstruction demonstrations. It also implicitly draws a boundary on what Track A observations warrant (they are not evidence of decoding/interior-reconstruction capability), though that "false positive" warning is more explicit in the broader dual-track description than in this single statement.

    What is Novel: The core phenomenon-level content (Track A Hawking-like signatures are achievable via effective-metric engineering and local measurements, without TFD/two-copy/decoding primitives) is already explicit and established in several Track A-focused papers and reviews, so it is not novel as an empirical claim. Relative to some broader black-hole/quantum-information reviews that discuss both analogue Hawking phenomena and holographic decoding/teleportation but do not explicitly contrast their resource requirements, the statement functions mainly as an articulated cross-context separation that is often derivable from existing demonstrations rather than newly discovered.

    Explanatory / Mechanistic — Derivable Unstated¶

    What is Known: Across Track-A analogue/curved-spacetime-simulation papers, Hawking/Unruh-like signatures are already mechanistically attributed to engineered effective metrics/horizons (via flow/propagation-speed/coupling profiles) plus local spectral/correlation readout (Bogoliubov pair creation, tunneling/detailed-balance temperatures set by surface gravity). Across Track-B holographic/information-theoretic simulation papers, probing scrambling/teleportation/decoding explicitly requires global entanglement structure (often TFD-like), multi-copy access, and control primitives such as time reversal or U* (or equivalents) to implement/diagnose decoding.

    What is Introduced: The statement makes explicit a resource–mechanism claim: for Track A, reproducing Hawking-like local observables is governed primarily by metric/profile engineering and measurement resolution and does not require Track-B decoding primitives (TFD preparation, two-copy resources, U*/time-reversal access). It elevates this into a feasibility-bottleneck assertion (analog control/readout dominates, not decoding complexity).

    What is Novel: The underlying kinematic mechanism (metric engineering → horizon kinematics → thermal/partner-correlation signatures) is explicitly established in multiple Track-A works, so that portion is not novel. The more novel contribution is the explicit resource-bottleneck contrast —stating as a principle that Track-A demonstrations are independent of decoding/TFD/U* primitives that are central in Track-B—since in the assessed corpus this contrast is typically only implicit or inferable by juxtaposing Track-A and Track-B discussions rather than stated as a mechanistic necessity/sufficiency claim.

    Unification — Derivable Unstated¶

    What is Known: Across analogue/kinematic-horizon work, Track-A-style demonstrations are typically framed as engineering an effective metric/horizon and validating via local spectra/correlators (thermal/Hawking-like emission, pair correlations), without invoking TFD states, two-copy protocols, or U* access (e.g., BEC Hawking/Unruh-style simulators and QFT-in-curved-space mappings). Separately, holographic/SYK/decoding papers treat scrambling/teleportation/Page-curve-type goals as relying on specialized entanglement resources and decoding/complex conjugation–like primitives, but usually without explicitly contrasting these requirements against analogue-horizon feasibility as a formal organizing principle.

    What is Introduced: The statement makes the resource/validation separation explicit: for Track A, the primary requirements are effective-metric (horizon) engineering plus sufficiently resolved local measurements, and decoding primitives (TFD preparation, two-copy resources, U* access) are not required. It turns this into an operational feasibility claim (dominant bottleneck = analogue control/readout, not decoding complexity) intended to prevent conflating Track-A signatures with Track-B capabilities.

    What is Novel: The core content is largely a synthesis of established practice—many papers implicitly assume Track A can be validated locally and Track B requires decoding-like resources—yet the explicit, principle-level resource taxonomy is generally not stated as an organizing claim in the surveyed literature. Some broad/contrastive papers provide enough ingredients that this separation is plausibly derivable by a careful reader, but the aggregated evidence does not show it as an explicit, established unifying statement; thus the novelty is mainly in explicitly elevating and formalizing the separation rather than in introducing a new technical result.

    Generalization / Scope Expansion — Explicit Established¶

    What is Known: Across the analogue-gravity / QFT-in-curved-spacetime simulation literature, Track A-style aims are explicitly framed as fixed-background (kinematic) simulations where the core requirement is engineering an effective metric/horizon via controllable couplings/profiles and validating via local spectra/correlators. Multiple broad treatments already cover the relevant platform classes and implicitly (and sometimes explicitly) place the bottleneck on analogue control and readout, not on holographic-style decoding resources.

    What is Introduced: The statement elevates this into an explicit, cross-platform feasibility principle: Hawking-like kinematic signatures in effective-metric analogues do not require thermofield-double preparation, two-copy resources, or U* (time-reversal/decoder) access, so feasibility is dominated by metric/profile engineering and measurement resolution.

    What is Novel: The strongest counterevidence is that essentially this same Track A feasibility framing (metric engineering + local measurements as the dominant requirements) is already explicitly established in core analogue-gravity and QFT-in-curved-space simulation references, making the statement largely non-novel within its stated domain. Some review-style sources enumerate platforms/regimes without stating a single, uniform 'resource-dominance' conclusion, so the exact cross-modality packaging is at most a synthesis; but given explicit prior articulations of the kinematic-analogue requirements, the aggregated evidence supports classifying the statement as established rather than a substantive new scope expansion.

    Constraint / Limitation — Derivable Unstated¶

    What is Known: Across multiple analogue/Track-A simulator papers and reviews, it is explicit and established that these platforms implement QFT kinematics on fixed effective metrics (no dynamical Einstein equations/backreaction) and that Hawking-like thermality/partner-correlation claims are limited by finite-size/dispersion, noise, stimulated backgrounds, and measurement/tomography assumptions. Separately, Track-B (holographic/decoding/teleportation) work explicitly treats interior reconstruction/decoding as requiring extra primitives (e.g., TFD-like entanglement, two copies, time reversal or U* access) and shows that thermality alone is not sufficient for information-recovery claims.

    What is Introduced: The statement sharpens these points into an operational resource-bottleneck rule: Track A feasibility is dominated by metric (horizon) engineering plus local spectral/correlator readout, and does not require Track-B decoding primitives (TFD/two-copy/U*). It also functions as a diagnostic constraint against category errors—inferring decoding/interior-reconstruction capability from Track-A Hawking-like observations (or using Track-B teleportation-like signals to claim a geometric horizon).

    What is Novel: The kinematics-not-dynamics limitation and the dominance of analog control/measurement systematics for Track-A signatures are not novel (they are repeatedly explicit and treated as established in analogue-gravity experiment/simulation papers). The more novel element is the explicitly stated, actionable separation in terms of required experimental primitives/resources ("no TFD/two-copy/U* needed for Track A" and "don’t validate Track B with Track-A signatures"), which is often only implicit/derivable from the combination of analogue-gravity caveats and holographic/decoding resource analyses—though some holography-adjacent papers already effectively demonstrate closely related “thermality/signature insufficiency” warnings.

    Conceptual Reframing / Abstraction — Explicit Established¶

    What is Known: Across the analogue-gravity / QFT-in-curved-spacetime simulation literature, Track-A-style goals are commonly framed as kinematic simulations on fixed effective metrics where the key experimental task is engineering an effective horizon (via spatial/temporal coupling/velocity profiles) and validating via local spectra and correlation measurements. Several works explicitly stress the kinematic-vs-dynamical distinction and, at least implicitly, that observing Hawking-like thermality/correlations does not entail the holographic decoding/interior-reconstruction capabilities that arise in information-theoretic black-hole discussions.

    What is Introduced: The statement compresses this into an explicit resource-bottleneck claim: Track A does not require TFD preparation, two-copy resources, or U* (time-reversal/decoding) access, so feasibility is dominated by analogue control over coupling profiles and measurement/readout resolution. In the broader Dual-Track theory context, this serves as the Track-A side of a prescriptive taxonomy warning against importing Track-B validation/resources into Track-A assessments.

    What is Novel: Given the least-novel evidence papers, the core reframing that kinematic-horizon simulations are primarily about metric/coupling engineering plus local readout (rather than decoding primitives) appears already explicit and established in the analogue-gravity and curved-spacetime-simulation framing. The incremental novelty is mainly in making the contrast with holographic/decoding primitives maximally explicit as an operational feasibility criterion, but this is better characterized as a consolidation/foregrounding of standard practice than a new abstraction.

    Empirical Synthesis / Meta-Regularity — Explicit Established¶

    What is Known: Across analogue-gravity/fluids syntheses, it is already explicitly treated as established that Hawking-like spectra and partner correlations are kinematic QFT-on-curved-background signatures that can be reproduced by engineering an effective horizon/metric and measuring local spectra/correlators, within the relevant low-energy/hydrodynamic validity regimes. Separately, holography-to-simulator/SYK reviews and experiments treat decoding/teleportation/scrambling diagnostics as requiring qualitatively different resources (e.g., specialized protocols and often multi-copy/TFD-like ingredients) but typically do not codify an explicit cross-platform rule connecting this to analogue-horizon validation.

    What is Introduced: The statement elevates an operational meta-guideline for Track A: feasibility and validation are dominated by metric/coupling profile engineering and local readout resolution, and do not require Track-B-style primitives (TFD preparation, two-copy resources, or U* access). In the broader framing, it implicitly supports a cross-track caution: local kinematic success in analogue horizons should not be interpreted as evidence for information-theoretic interior-reconstruction capability.

    What is Novel: The Track-A-specific core regularity (metric engineering + local correlators suffice; decoding primitives are not required) is not novel because it is explicitly synthesized as standard in the analogue-gravity review literature. The only potentially novel element is the cross-literature elevation of this as part of a two-track, non-transferable-diagnostics guideline; across the assessed corpus this appears mostly as a higher-level synthesis that is at best derivable from combining separate literatures rather than being explicitly established in any single place.

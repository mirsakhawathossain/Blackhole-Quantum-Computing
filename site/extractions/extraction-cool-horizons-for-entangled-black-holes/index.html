    Powerful quantum computer acting on Hawking radiation (thought-experiment of Alice)¶

    Field Value
    name_short Alice's quantum computer (thought-experiment)
    name_full Powerful quantum computer acting on Hawking radiation (thought-experiment of Alice)
    brief_description A hypothetical, arbitrarily powerful quantum computer that Alice uses to act unitarily on the Hawking radiation (or on a distant black hole) to distill/build a second black hole in a specific entangled (thermofield) state, thereby altering the interior (creating or removing a firewall) in the thought-experiments.
    citation_title Cool horizons for entangled black holes
    mention_or_use mention
    target_system_or_model Evaporating/entangled black hole plus its Hawking radiation; two-sided eternal AdS black hole (thermofield double) as target state
    black_hole_phenomena_targeted Preparation of thermofield-double entangled state; reconstruction/alteration of black hole interior (smooth horizon vs firewall); transmission of messages through ER bridge; entanglement structure of black hole + radiation
    simulation_paradigm Conceptual use of an abstract, ideal quantum computer to perform arbitrary unitary operations on the radiation / distant black hole; not a concrete digital/analog simulation protocol
    quantum_hardware_platform platform-agnostic (thought-experiment assumes ideal universal quantum computer)
    encoding_and_mapping None
    algorithm_or_protocol Abstract statement: apply an appropriate global unitary U_A on Alice's share (radiation or left CFT) to produce the thermofield-double (TFD) or other desired entangled state; no explicit algorithm (e.g., no circuit decomposition, no Trotterization) is provided
    resource_estimates None
    noise_and_error_mitigation None
    key_results_or_demonstrations Conceptual claim only: with an arbitrarily powerful quantum computer Alice can (by acting solely on her radiation) produce a second black hole in the special entangled state (TFD) with the original, thereby restoring a smooth interior or sending messages (including firewalls) into Bob's black hole; no numerical simulation or experimental demonstration is provided.
    validation_and_benchmarks Argumentative / semiclassical reasoning using Penrose diagrams, thermofield-double construction, and AMPS-style thought experiments; no computational or numerical benchmarks presented.
    claimed_feasibility Authors explicitly assume arbitrary quantum-computational power for the thought experiment but note possible limits on computation (cite [18]); they do not claim near-term experimental feasibility and treat the operation as idealized/in-principle only.
    limitations_and_open_problems No concrete procedure or circuit given; no resource or complexity analysis in this paper; assumes ability to collect and coherently manipulate the full Hawking radiation (extremely challenging); ignores decoherence, noise, and computational-complexity bounds except to note they exist; does not address how to physically implement the required unitary on realistic hardware.
    complexity_or_hardness_arguments The paper notes and explicitly sets aside possible limits on quantum computation (ref. [18]) rather than proving complexity bounds; no formal BQP/QMA statements are made here, but the thought experiment relies on extremely difficult (presumably exponentially hard) decoding operations.
    theory_context_keywords ER=EPR, thermofield double, AMPS paradox, entanglement, black hole information, scrambling, holography
    citations_to_prior_work AMPS(S) paradox (refs [11,12] in the paper); reference to limits on quantum computation (ref [18]); thermofield-double construction and prior AdS/CFT discussions (refs [6,10]); general holographic context (Ryu-Takayanagi [23], Van Raamsdonk [24])

    Preparation of the thermofield-double (TFD) / entangled two-black-hole state by unitary operations on radiation¶

    Field Value
    name_short TFD preparation via radiation unitary (concept)
    name_full Preparation of the thermofield-double (TFD) / entangled two-black-hole state by unitary operations on radiation
    brief_description The paper discusses preparing the specific maximally-entangled thermofield-double state (which geometrically corresponds to two black holes connected by an Einstein-Rosen bridge) by acting with a unitary on one subsystem (the radiation or the left CFT), as an abstract state-preparation goal using a quantum computer.
    citation_title Cool horizons for entangled black holes
    mention_or_use mention
    target_system_or_model Thermofield-double eternal AdS black hole state (TFD); implicitly the system of a black hole entangled with its radiation (black hole + radiation Hilbert space)
    black_hole_phenomena_targeted Geometry of the interior (ER bridge), smooth horizon vs firewall dichotomy, ability to exchange messages through the wormhole after TFD preparation, entanglement structure and its geometric manifestation
    simulation_paradigm Abstract state-preparation thought-experiment (preparation of a many-body entangled state that is dual to a two-sided black hole); not given as a concrete variational or trotterized protocol
    quantum_hardware_platform platform-agnostic
    encoding_and_mapping None
    algorithm_or_protocol Stated at high level: apply a (potentially extremely complex) unitary on one subsystem to convert an arbitrary maximally entangled state with the same reduced density matrices into the precise TFD state; no explicit algorithm, mapping, or circuit depth given.
    resource_estimates None
    noise_and_error_mitigation None
    key_results_or_demonstrations Conceptual assertion: such a unitary exists in principle and can restore the TFD (rejuvenate phases) to recreate the smooth ER bridge; no constructive protocol or demonstration is given.
    validation_and_benchmarks Validated only by theoretical semiclassical arguments about the relation between the TFD state and the eternal black hole geometry; the paper uses Penrose diagrams and the thermofield formalism rather than computational benchmarks.
    claimed_feasibility Treated as an in-principle possibility requiring idealized computational resources; authors explicitly acknowledge computational limits may obstruct realization but do not analyze them here.
    limitations_and_open_problems No constructive method to find the required unitary; absence of resource/complexity estimates; difficulty of preparing exact TFD from generic entangled radiation; how to verify interior reconstruction operationally is left open.
    complexity_or_hardness_arguments Paper references (and sets aside) computational complexity concerns (ref [18]); suggests decoding radiation into TFD likely requires extremely complex operations but provides no formal complexity lower bounds.
    theory_context_keywords thermofield double, entanglement=geometry, ER=EPR, AMPS, holography, black hole interior reconstruction, scrambling
    citations_to_prior_work Thermofield construction and AdS eternal black hole discussions (refs [6,10]); AMPS(S) paradox (refs [11,12]); computational-limitation discussion referenced (ref [18]); Ryu-Takayanagi [23], Van Raamsdonk [24]

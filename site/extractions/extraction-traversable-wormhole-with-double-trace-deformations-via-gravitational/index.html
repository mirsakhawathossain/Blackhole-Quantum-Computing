    Holographic teleportation / thermofield-double teleportation protocol realized in SYK-type models (theoretical proposals)¶

    Field Value
    name_short holographic-Teleportation-in-SYK
    name_full Holographic teleportation / thermofield-double teleportation protocol realized in SYK-type models (theoretical proposals)
    brief_description Theoretical proposals show that the Gao–Jafferis–Wall traversable wormhole protocol maps to a quantum-teleportation-like protocol between two entangled copies (thermofield double) and that SYK-type models can realize this mapping; the present paper references these proposals as motivation for laboratory simulations.
    citation_title
    mention_or_use mention
    target_system_or_model Thermofield-double state of two entangled many-body systems, in practice modeled/theorized by SYK-type models
    black_hole_phenomena_targeted Traversable wormhole opening (ANEC violation), teleportation of quantum information through an entangled pair interpreted as wormhole traversal, scrambling and late-time power-law tails in correlators
    simulation_paradigm Conceptual mapping to laboratory quantum systems (the paper cites theoretical-to-experimental proposals but does not present a simulation implementation itself); primarily digital quantum simulation proposals and table-top analogs are referenced in the introduction.
    quantum_hardware_platform platform-agnostic (paper mentions the possibility of tabletop experiments and quantum-computer implementations, without platform detail)
    encoding_and_mapping Not specified in this paper; the general mapping discussed in the literature is: two entangled copies in a thermofield-double correspond to two asymptotic boundaries, and double-trace boundary couplings implement the nonlocal interaction — specific qubit encodings or fermion-to-qubit mappings are not presented here.
    algorithm_or_protocol Teleportation-through-wormhole protocol via double-trace boundary coupling (Gao–Jafferis–Wall framework) implemented in SYK-type models; the paper uses this as conceptual motivation but does not detail quantum-circuit level algorithms.
    resource_estimates No resource estimates are provided in this paper for realizing the teleportation protocol on quantum hardware.
    noise_and_error_mitigation Not discussed in this paper for experimental/theoretical SYK realizations.
    key_results_or_demonstrations The paper uses the holographic-teleportation mapping as motivation and context; it does not present experimental demonstrations, but notes prior simulation attempts exist (cited) and have produced debatable results.
    validation_and_benchmarks No direct validation is performed in this work for quantum-simulation proposals; the paper validates its own gravitational calculations by comparison with previous holographic results (scalar/vector cases) but not against quantum-simulation data.
    claimed_feasibility Authors suggest that holographic teleportation could be approachable in tabletop or quantum-computer experiments in the future, motivating exploration, but they do not provide quantitative feasibility estimates.
    limitations_and_open_problems Open problems noted include: viability of the duality for small/simplified SYK models used in experiments, how to realize genuine gravitational dynamics in laboratory systems, the probe-limit constraints on information transfer, and verification of quantum-gravity signatures in finite-size noisy devices.
    complexity_or_hardness_arguments No explicit complexity-theoretic statements are made regarding teleportation protocol simulation hardness.
    theory_context_keywords Gao–Jafferis–Wall traversable wormhole protocol, thermofield double, SYK model, holographic teleportation, ANEC violation, hydrodynamic modes, diffusion vs. propagating modes
    citations_to_prior_work The paper cites the mapping between traversable wormholes and teleportation (Gao–Jafferis–Wall and follow-up works) and explicit SYK modeling references (cited as [21] and [25]); the manuscript notes experimental/theoretical debate (cited as [26]).

## Theories

- [Operator-Size Phase Coherence as the Mechanism for Wormhole Teleportation Signals (refined)](theories/operator-size-phase-coherence-as-the-mechanism-for-wormhole-teleportation-signal.md) _(theory)_
  This theory addresses when traversable-wormhole-inspired teleportation experiments/simulations on quantum devices should be interpreted as reflecting a specifically holographic/SYK-like mechanism ("teleportation-by-size-winding") versus more generic mechanisms (e.g., peaked-size distributions or simple thermalization) that can also yield teleportation-like signatures. The central claim is that a distinctive holographic mechanism requires (i) a well-defined operator-size decomposition (e.g., Pauli/Majorana strings), (ii) phase coherence across operator-size sectors so that a complex size-resolved quantity q(l) has an approximately linear phase in l ("size winding") and a coherence ratio R(l)=|q(l)|/P(l) near 1 over the dominant size support, and (iii) a two-sided coupling e^{i\mu V} that effectively cancels or reverses this winding for the appropriate sign of \mu, yielding a strong sign-asymmetry in mutual information/teleportation fidelity. In contrast, protocols can show teleportation in small-N or commuting/learned Hamiltonians even without coherent winding, via non-holographic mechanisms (peaked-size/thermalization), and therefore teleportation signals alone are not a sufficient certificate of holographic wormhole dynamics.
- [Horizon-Encoded Hopping Universality for Hawking-like Thermality](theories/horizon-encoded-hopping-universality-for-hawking-like-thermality.md) _(theory)_
  In a broad class of 1+1D analogue-gravity constructions where a static metric function f(x) (or equivalent local wave-speed profile) is encoded into a site-dependent nearest-neighbor hopping profile κn (fermionic/XY or bosonic hopping), the mere presence of a horizon (a zero of f, so κ changes sign or passes through zero in the appropriate mapping) generically yields an approximately thermal (Boltzmann) energy distribution for excitations transmitted to the exterior region, with an effective temperature set primarily by the local gradient f'(xh) (surface-gravity analogue) rather than by global equilibration. The theory explains why both superconducting-chain ‘lattice black hole’ experiments and classical lattice numerics recover exponential spectra, and it predicts structured, controllable deviations driven by finite chain length (IR cutoffs and reflections), coordinate choice (which modes are represented and whether the process appears as tunneling vs crossing), dispersion/truncation, and whether the observed radiation is stimulated (seeded) vs spontaneous (vacuum).
- [Dual-Track Black-Hole Simulation Principle (Kinematic Horizons vs Holographic Information)](theories/dual-track-black-hole-simulation-principle-kinematic-horizons-vs-holographic-inf.md) _(theory)_
  This theory posits that the feasibility of simulating black holes on quantum platforms bifurcates into two largely independent tracks with different success criteria and resource bottlenecks. Track A (kinematic horizon/QFT-in-curved-space analogues) simulates field propagation on a fixed effective metric (e.g., sonic horizons in BECs, SQUID transmission lines, superconducting lattice horizons, Dirac-in-curved-space mappings) and is validated by local spectra/correlations (thermal emission, partner correlations, redshift, tunneling rates). Track B (holographic/information-theoretic black holes) targets many-body systems believed to have gravitational duals (SYK/JT-like) and focuses on scrambling, Page transitions, decoding, and traversable wormhole teleportation; it requires thermofield-double-like entanglement, multi-copy operations, and often time reversal. Mixing validation criteria across tracks leads to false positives: e.g., a thermal Hawking-like spectrum in Track A does not imply interior reconstruction ability in Track B, while teleportation-like signals in Track B may arise without any geometric horizon in Track A.
- [Scrambling-Limited Simulatability of Black-Hole Information Tasks (revised)](theories/scrambling-limited-simulatability-of-black-hole-information-tasks-revised.md) _(theory)_
  This theory explains feasibility of quantum-computer simulations of black-hole *information* phenomena (as opposed to purely kinematic horizon analogues) through a hierarchical requirement on controllable access to both forward scrambling and its conjugate/inverse, plus entangled two-copy resources and precise subsystem partitioning. Evidence supports a three-tier hierarchy: (i) forward-only chaos/thermal diagnostics (OTOCs/level statistics/thermal spectra) are achievable with approximate Hamiltonians and moderate control; (ii) wormhole-teleportation-style demonstrations and Hayden–Preskill-style toy recovery require two-copy entanglement resources, calibrated couplings, and at least partial time reversal or conjugate dynamics; (iii) scalable high-fidelity decoding or interior reconstruction generically requires either exponentially small postselection probability or depth that grows rapidly with message size and/or code-subspace size, and is highly sensitive to noise. The theory also clarifies a key caveat: some teleportation-like signals can be produced by non-holographic mechanisms in specially structured or commuting models, so observing a signal is not sufficient to infer "semiclassical bulk" fidelity.
- [Dual-Route Simulatability: Metric-Engineered QFT vs Holographic Scrambler Emulation](theories/dual-route-simulatability-metric-engineered-qft-vs-holographic-scrambler-emulati.md) _(theory)_
  Quantum hardware can access black-hole physics through two structurally different routes with different scaling and validation: (1) Metric-engineered QFT-on-fixed-background simulations, where spacetime enters as spatially/time-dependent couplings in an effective low-dimensional field theory (e.g., site-dependent hopping encoding f(x) and horizons; squeezing Hamiltonians encoding Rindler/Unruh transformations; Dirac-in-curved-space mapped to trapped-ion QRM). These can yield direct Hawking/Unruh thermality and propagation/redshift signatures, often at single-particle or linearized-field level. (2) Holographic scrambler emulation, where one simulates a boundary many-body system believed to have a gravity dual (SYK/JT-inspired), and probes black-hole-like behavior via scrambling, OTOCs, teleportation-through-wormhole protocols, and decoding. Route (1) is kinematic and comparatively near-term; route (2) targets information-theoretic and interior-reconstruction signatures but requires large Hilbert spaces, TFD/EPR resources, and deep circuits or analog engineering of all-to-all randomness. The theory predicts that these routes are not interchangeable: matching a Hawking-like spectrum in route (1) does not imply success at decoding/Page-curve tasks in route (2), and conversely, demonstrating teleportation-by-size in route (2) does not validate semiclassical Hawking emission in route (1).
- [Operational Equivalence Classes for Quantum Black-Hole Simulation (revised)](theories/operational-equivalence-classes-for-quantum-black-hole-simulation-revised.md) _(theory)_
  A claim that a quantum computer (digital or analog) “simulates a black hole” is only meaningful relative to an operational equivalence class: (i) a specified target set of black-hole-relevant observables O (e.g., thermal Hawking/Unruh spectra and correlations; OTOC/level-statistics chaos diagnostics; teleportation-through-wormhole correlators; Hayden–Preskill decoding fidelity/success; Page-curve-like entropy behavior), (ii) an explicit mapping M that turns those observables into measurable quantities in a controllable quantum system, and (iii) a validation regime W where the theoretical relations among observables that define the intended phenomenon are expected to hold (universality window). Evidence supports that multiple inequivalent equivalence classes are currently accessible: (A) kinematic fixed-background QFT analogues (acoustic/photonic/superconducting/trapped-ion mappings of horizons, Unruh thermality, and single-particle curved-spacetime propagation), and (B) holographic/quantum-information analogues (SYK and related scramblers; traversable-wormhole teleportation protocols; decoding protocols; coarse-grained wormhole-as-channel formulations). The theory explains why different experiments legitimately claim “black-hole simulation” while targeting different O, and why stronger claims (interior reconstruction, unitary evaporation) require additional capabilities (TFD/EPR preparation, two-copy access, ability to implement U and U*, postselection or amplification, and a verifiable radiation partition). It also incorporates the ensemble-vs-single-instance issue: when gravitational path integrals/replica wormholes compute ensemble-averaged quantities, a hardware simulator implementing a single Hamiltonian instance may match only after specifying the relevant averaging/typicality assumptions and code-subspace restrictions.
